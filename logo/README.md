# Vettic Labs — logo candidates

Ten minimalist marks, ranked. 1024×1024 transparent PNGs, ink #1f2a44.
Source SVGs: see logo-presentation artifact / session scratchpad.

1.png — **The Footnote** (wildcard-footnote-asterisk): The typographic footnote: a bold three-stroke asterisk resting above its footnote rule — the universal mark for 'there is reasoning attached, read it.' It literalizes 'a score you can read and challenge' without touching a V, a check, or a stamp, and the asterisk-plus-rule pairing (rather than a bare asterisk) makes it ownable.
2.png — **The Caliper Point** (readable-score-caliper-point): Two square editorial brackets — caliper jaws — holding a single heavy point: the candidate's score, measured but not crushed, with air on every side so it stays open to inspection. Brackets belong to the dossier's typeset world without being type, and the mark doubles as a [•] annotation an editor could query.
3.png — **The Assay Notch** (negative-space-assay-notch): A solid coin with one machined square notch cut at twelve o'clock — the assayer's cut, the historical practice of taking a precise sample from a coin to certify what it is made of. One shape, one cut, drawable from memory; the calmest possible statement that Vettic tests the material, not the surface.
4.png — **The Strike** (stamp-tilted-strike): A thick rounded-square stamp frame struck 7 degrees off true, with a chunky flat-apex V inside — the deliberate imperfection of the tilt is what says 'a human pressed this,' turning the badge-with-V trope into an impression, not an emblem. The flat-bottomed V is drawn as press-cut geometry, not a checkmark.
5.png — **The Notched Seal** (stamp-notched-seal): A heavy circular seal ring with a V-shaped registration notch cut cleanly out of its crown — the V exists only as negative space, the cut a rubber stamp leaves, so the seal reads first and the letterform is discovered second. Nobody in vetting owns 'the V is the notch in the seal.'
6.png — **The Open Dossier** (dossier-folded-open): A manila folder seen edge-on, pressed open — the trough of the open folder is the V, and the small index tab on its rim is what keeps it a folder rather than a checkmark. The V is never drawn; it is the space the dossier opens up for inspection.
7.png — **The Plinth** (monogram-shared-plinth): Two identical italic L's, one mirrored: each is a slanted stem with a horizontal foot, and the two feet overlap into a single full-width baseline while the two stems rise apart to spell the V. One glyph, rotated and shared, builds both letters — the dossier's V standing on the desk it is read at.
8.png — **The Punched File** (dossier-punched): The filed dossier itself: a dog-eared sheet with one bold circle punched clean through where the VETTED stamp landed — the site's single theatrical beat made permanent in the paper. One silhouette, one hole; the judgment is part of the document's anatomy, not decoration on it.
9.png — **The Overrun Gauge** (readable-score-overrun-gauge): A gauge dial whose needle is deliberately too long — it pokes clean through the scale, becoming a handle you could grab and push back. The dial is the machine's measurement; the overrun needle is the human's right to challenge it, pinned at a confident ~80% reading rather than a boastful 100%.
10.png — **The Set Square** (monogram-set-square): An L drawn as a thick drafting bracket (stem plus baseline) with the V's right arm as a bold diagonal brace dropping from the top-right corner into the L's inner corner — the L's stem doubles as the V's left arm, and the whole mark inscribes an implied square. It reads as a set square: a measuring tool, not a checkmark — judgment you can verify against a straight edge.
